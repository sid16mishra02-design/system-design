# System Design: a structured learning path

A category-based curriculum combining **Karan Pratap Singh’s complete course** with **Ashish Pratap Singh’s topic resources, tradeoffs, interview framework, and problem bank**. Learn in order, visualize the ideas, and finish with designs you can explain yourself.

## Start here

1. Set your study window in the [22-week schedule](SCHEDULE.md). Default: 60–90 minutes, five study days plus a review day and a rest day.
2. Start at category 01 below and follow the numbered topics. The schedule gives the exact week and day.
3. On a course topic page, read the linked source section first, then the short companion example when present. Original course text and diagrams remain intact in [sources/karan](sources/karan/README.md).
4. Keep a [daily note](templates/DAILY.md) and update the [progress tracker](PROGRESS.md). Move unfinished work forward when you need more time.

You do not need to finish a topic in one sitting. Move on when you can explain it simply, draw its flow, and describe a failure or tradeoff. Follow this one path; open the extra library to answer a specific question.

## Finish line

Complete the foundations, produce five independent case-study worksheets, then explain one unfamiliar design with capacity estimates, a complete request flow, a failure response, and two justified tradeoffs. The [easy/medium/hard problem bank](practice/PROBLEMS.md) remains available for continued practice.

## Content preservation

The original course and resource README files are copied unchanged, with their diagrams and licenses. Categories and examples live in separate files. Course explanations have **0% source-text edits**; each topic’s added teaching text is capped at **30% of its original topic word count**, below your 20% edit / 30% addition limits. [See the per-topic audit](CONTENT-AUDIT.md).

Resource-only entries retain links rather than reproducing external articles. No percentage claim is made about those third-party pages. Schedule, navigation, and worksheets are separate planning material, not expansions of source topics.
